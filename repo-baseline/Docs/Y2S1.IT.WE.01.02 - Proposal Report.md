1. Introduction

The rapid growth of online retails has transformed the way businesses reach
customers, moving away from physical stores and into digital marketplaces which
are accessible anytime, anywhere. However, most of these digital marketplaces are
built around a single seller whose managing their own catalogue, orders and a set
of customers whom they interact with in isolation. This creates a distasteful
experience for both the customers, and the merchants. Customers would need
manage communication with each seller individually, visit multiple separate
websites just to compare products and track each order separately. This would
create an unpleasant situation for merchants, particularly small and medium sized
sellers who often lacks an affordable, ready-made platform where they can list the
product without having to build an entire system of their own.

To resolve this issue, a unified, multi-merchant platform must be addressed, by
bringing several independent sellers together under one system, giving customers
a single point of access to a wider range of products while giving the merchants a
shared infrastructure to manage their business individually.

This project proposes the development of a web-based multi-merchant shopping
mall system built using Java, Spring Boot and JSP with Microsoft SQL Server as
the underlying database that allows multiple merchants to register and manage
their own stores while customers to be given the ability to browse, purchase, and
interact with products from different sellers, rate the products within a single
cohesive platform. The system is designed to streamline the core processes of an
online marketplace, from merchant and product management to cart and order
handling, to reviews and customer support within one integrated application.

In addition, the project serves as a practical application of software engineering
principles by following the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), adopting
the Agile development methodology, and applying UML modelling techniques to
design a well-structured, modular, and maintainable system.

•  To provide a centralized system for managing all system actors and their
roles including registration, authentication, and role-based access for

1.3 Stakeholders

There are 3 major stakeholders in this system. Merchants are business owners or
sellers who use the platform to register their store list and manage their products
and fulfil customer orders. Customers are the end users of the system who browse
the product catalogue and place order. Finally, the Admin oversees the overall
platform, ensuring merchants and content comply with platform policies and acts
as an intermediary in resolving disputes or customer inquiries. Together, these
three roles interact within the system to enable a functioning multi-merchant
marketplace.

3. Functional Requirements

The system’s functional requirements are organized according to the six main functions
depicted above. These requirements describe the key features and capabilities that the
system is planning to provide.

•  The system shall allow merchants to register, verify, and manage their shop

profiles.

•  The system shall enforce role-based access, ensuring merchants, customers, and

platform employees can only perform actions permitted for their role.

•  The system shall allow regular customers to create personal accounts on the

system.

•  The system shall allow merchants to add, edit, and manage their product catalog.
•  The system shall allow customers to add products to a cart and manage cart items

before checkout.

•  The system shall allow customers to complete secure payments and track orders

through delivery.

•  The system shall allow customers to submit ratings and reviews for products they

have purchased, with reviews restricted to verified buyers.

•  The system shall calculate and display aggregated rating data, including per-

product distribution and overall merchant reputation scores.

•  The system shall notify or flag products for platform employees when ratings fall

below a defined threshold.

•  The system shall allow customers to submit inquiries or complaints, and platform

employees to track and resolve them.

6. Six Major Functions.

3.1 User & Stakeholder Management

This function serves as the centralized identity and role administration engine for the
platform, managing registration, verification, and access control for every type of system
users - customers, merchants, and platform employees. Rather than treating account
management as a background utility, this function governs how each role is onboarded,
verified, and permitted to act within the system, forming the foundation that all other
functions depend on.

•  Allows customers to register and manage a personal account
•  Allows merchants to register, submit shop details, and manage their shop profile

7

•  Allows platform employees to verify, approve, or suspend merchant accounts to

maintain marketplace trust

•  Enforces role-based access control, ensuring each actor can only perform actions

permitted for their role

3.2 Product catalogue management

This function provides customers with an organized and searchable view of product from
all merchants on the platform, making it easy to discover items across categories and
sellers.

•  The system categorizes the products (e.g., electronics, clothing, groceries)
•  Customers can search product by name and keyword
•  Customers can filter product by category, price range, or merchant
•  The system displays a detailed product page
•  Customers can view products from multiple merchants under a unified catalogue

view

3.3 Shopping cart handling

This function lets customers collect products they intend to purchase, adjust quantities
and review their selections before proceeding to checkout.

•  Customers can add products to the cart from the catalogue
•  Customers can update the quantity of items in the cart
•  Customers can remove the items from the cart
•  The system displays the cart total before the checkout
•  Customers can view the cart before checkout

3.4 Payment and Order tracking

This function manages the process of converting a customer’s cart into a confirmed order,
handling payment and allowing both customers and merchants to track order progress

•  Customers can checkout process that converts cart contents into an order
•  Payment option (redirects to the payment gateway)
•  The system lets the customer know of the order confirmation and generates a

summary

•  Customers can track the order status (e.g., pending, processing, shipped,

delivered)

•  Customers can view their order history
•  The system allows merchant-side order management (View orders placed for the

product, update status)

3.5 Product Feedback & Reputation Analytics

This function gives customers a trustworthy way to evaluate products and merchants,
while giving merchants and platform employees meaningful insight into product and
shop performance. By combining verified feedback and proactive quality monitoring, this
function turns customer feedback into a measurable reputation signal across the platform.

•  Allows customers to submit a star rating and written review for a product,

restricted to verified purchasers only

•  Calculates and displays average rating and full rating distribution per product
•  Aggregates individual product ratings into an overall merchant reputation score
•  The system lists individual reviews on the product detail page
•  The system restricts reviews only to the customers who purchased that product
•  Allows merchants to publicly respond to customer reviews
•  Automatically flags products for platform employee attention when ratings fall

below a defined threshold

3.6 Customer support and inquiry system

This function gives customers a direct channel to raise inquiries or issues and receive
timely responses, ensuring support is not limited to the point of purchase.

•  Customers can submit inquiries or complaints tied to an order or product
•  Administrators can respond to the submitted inquiry and connect with the

merchant if it is necessary

•  Customers can view the status of the submitted inquiry
•  The system manages a basic FAQ section

8. System Limitations

8.1 Platform / Technical Constraints

•  The system is a web-based application, which is only accessible through standard
desktop/laptop browsers. No native mobile application is provided. While the
interface may be responsive to some extent, it is not optimized for small mobile
screens.

•  The system is built using Java, Spring Boot, and JSP, requiring a compatible Java

runtime environment and application server for deployment.

•  The system uses Microsoft SQL Server as its database, meaning deployment

requires an environment with MSSQL support therefore is not designed to run on
other database engines without modification.

•  The system is developed and tested in a local/development environment for

academic purposes and is not deployed to a production grade or cloud hosted
environment with real world traffic handling.

10

8.2 Functional Limitations

•  Payment processing is simulated, not integrated with a real third-party payment

gateway therefore no actual monetary transactions occur.

•  Delivery is not tracked in real time - order status updates are manually set by

merchants rather than integrated with courier/shipping APIs.

•  The system supports a single language and currency. Multi-language and multi-

currency support are not included.

•  Product feedback analytics are rule-based, not powered by machine learning. Low-
rating flagging uses a fixed threshold rather than predictive or sentiment-based
analysis.

•  The system does not include advanced features such as personalized product

recommendations, dynamic pricing, or AI-driven search.

8.3 Assumptions

•  Merchants are assumed to provide accurate and truthful information during

registration. The system's verification step is limited to basic checks and does not
perform legal/business registration validation.

•  Customers are assumed to have basic digital literacy and access to a device with a

modern web browser and internet connection.

•  The system assumes a moderate number of concurrent users appropriate for an

academic project demo, not enterprise-scale traffic.

•  Product and order data used during development/testing may be sample/dummy

data rather than real merchant inventories.

single, accessible platform, replacing fragmented, manual processes with a structured

functions, user and stakeholder management, product catalogue management, shopping
cart handling, order and payment handling, product feedback and reputation analytics,
